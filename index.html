  • Bachelor's Thesis project

    In this project, I worked with the BICS group on optimizing the analog to digital conversion operation for their spiking neural network-based sound source localization system. The original system encoding the sound data that came in from two different microphones via encoding the time different of when they came in. This time difference then triggered the release of spikes, that were fed into a spiking neuron hand tuned to determine the argument of the sound source with respect to the microphones. We attempted to optimize the filtering process of the raw analog data to digital spikes. In this end, we used a simulation of the TDE on both synthetic as well as freely available sound data. The thesis ultimately discovered that employed a very simply band-pass filter was sufficient to weakly encode the direction of sound information, and was also able to conclude that performance changed at different distances and heights, and that more tesing would need to be done before concluding this bandpass filter as sufficient.
